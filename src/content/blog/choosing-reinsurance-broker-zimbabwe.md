The relationship between an insurance company and its reinsurance broker is one of strategic partnership. The broker's effectiveness directly influences the insurer's cost of risk transfer, the breadth of coverage available, and the quality of support during claims. Choosing the right broker requires careful evaluation across several dimensions.

## Market Access and Relationships

A broker's primary function is to connect insurers with reinsurance capacity. The depth and breadth of a broker's relationships in the global reinsurance market are therefore the most important criteria. Top-tier brokers maintain strong relationships with Lloyd's syndicates, London company market carriers, European reinsurers, and regional African markets.

For Zimbabwean insurers, a broker with access to both international and African reinsurers is ideal. International markets offer deep capacity and sophisticated risk appetite, while regional reinsurers often have a better understanding of local conditions and may offer more competitive pricing for certain classes. A broker that can access both pools provides the insurer with optimal pricing and diversification of reinsurer credit risk.

## Technical Expertise and Analytical Capability

Reinsurance placement is a technically demanding discipline. The broker must be able to analyse the insurer's portfolio, identify loss trends, model catastrophe exposures, and structure programmes that provide appropriate protection at a sustainable cost. This requires qualified actuaries, underwriters, and risk modellers on the broking team.

Beyond placement, technical expertise is essential for policy wording review and negotiation. Reinsurance contracts are complex legal documents, and small differences in wording can have significant financial consequences at claims time. A broker with strong technical and legal skills ensures that the insurer's interests are fully protected.

## Local Knowledge and Claims Advocacy

While global market access is critical, local knowledge is equally important. A broker that understands Zimbabwe's regulatory environment, economic conditions, and risk landscape can present risks to reinsurers in a way that is both accurate and compelling. This local insight also enables the broker to advise insurers on emerging trends and market developments.

Claims advocacy is perhaps the most underappreciated aspect of a broker's role. When a major loss occurs, the broker becomes the insurer's representative in negotiations with reinsurers. A broker with strong claims handling capabilities, established relationships with reinsurance claims teams, and a track record of successful recoveries can make a material difference to the insurer's financial outcome.

## Why Independence Matters

An independent broker—one that is not owned by a reinsurer or part of a vertically integrated insurance group—acts solely in the insurer's interest. There is no conflict of interest, no pressure to place business with a related party, and no hidden agenda. The independent broker's only loyalty is to the client.

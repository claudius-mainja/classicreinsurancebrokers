The African risk landscape is evolving rapidly. As economies diversify and new industries emerge, insurers and their clients face exposures that fall well outside traditional property and casualty classes. Specialty risk insurance—covering areas such as cyber liability, political risk, aviation, and large-scale construction—requires deep technical knowledge, broad market access, and innovative structuring. A specialist reinsurance broker is often the key to securing effective coverage.

## Cyber Risk in the African Market

Cyber risk is no longer a concern confined to developed markets. African businesses are increasingly digitising their operations, handling sensitive customer data, and relying on interconnected systems. This digital transformation brings with it exposure to data breaches, ransomware attacks, and business interruption from system failures.

The cyber reinsurance market is still developing in Africa, with limited local capacity and pricing that can be volatile. A specialist broker helps insurers navigate this complexity by accessing international cyber markets—particularly Lloyd's and London company markets—where underwriters have the expertise to assess and price these risks. The broker also advises on policy wording, ensuring that coverage responds appropriately to the unique legal and regulatory environment in each African jurisdiction.

## Political Risk and Aviation

Political risk insurance is another area where specialty expertise is vital. Businesses operating across African borders face exposure to currency inconvertibility, expropriation, political violence, and contract frustration. These risks are difficult for standard insurers to assess and require dedicated political risk reinsurers with regional knowledge and geopolitical analysis capabilities.

Similarly, aviation insurance demands specialist handling. Airlines, maintenance facilities, and airport operators require coverage for hull, liability, and passenger risks that involve complex exposure modelling and large limits. The global aviation insurance market is highly specialised, and placements typically involve multiple reinsurers participating on layered programmes. Without a broker who understands the market dynamics and has established relationships with aviation underwriters, insurers cannot hope to secure optimal terms.

## Construction and Infrastructure

Across Africa, large-scale infrastructure projects are driving demand for construction and engineering insurance. These projects involve multiple contractors, complex liability matrices, and exposures that evolve over the construction period. Delays, design defects, and force majeure events add further complexity.

Specialty brokers add value by structuring wrap-up programmes, negotiating extensions of cover, and ensuring that the reinsurance tower provides sufficient limit for catastrophic loss scenarios. They also support claims advocacy, which is particularly important on complex construction losses where liability can be disputed among multiple parties.

For insurers looking to grow their specialty lines portfolio, partnering with a broker that has dedicated specialty teams, strong reinsurer relationships, and a track record in African risk placement is the most reliable path to success.

Treaty reinsurance is a cornerstone of the global insurance industry, providing the stability and capacity that primary insurers need to operate with confidence. For Zimbabwean insurers navigating a dynamic and sometimes volatile risk environment, understanding how treaty reinsurance works—and why a skilled broker is essential—is critical to long-term success.

## Proportional and Non-Proportional Structures

Treaty reinsurance arrangements generally fall into two broad categories: proportional and non-proportional. Under a proportional treaty, the reinsurer shares a fixed percentage of every premium and every loss within a defined class of business. This structure is straightforward and fosters strong alignment between the ceding insurer and the reinsurer, as both parties share equally in the underwriting outcome.

Non-proportional treaties, by contrast, are triggered only when losses exceed a specified threshold. The most common form is the excess-of-loss treaty, where the reinsurer covers losses above a defined retention level. This structure protects the insurer against catastrophic or aggregated loss events and is particularly valuable in markets exposed to severe weather, political instability, or large industrial claims.

Each structure serves a distinct purpose, and many Zimbabwean insurers maintain both proportional and non-proportional programmes to achieve comprehensive protection. Selecting the right blend requires deep analytical capability and a thorough understanding of the insurer's portfolio composition.

## Supporting Underwriting Capacity

One of the most significant benefits of treaty reinsurance is its ability to enhance underwriting capacity. By transferring a portion of risk to reinsurers, primary insurers can underwrite larger policies than their capital base would otherwise permit. This expanded capacity allows them to serve major clients—such as mining houses, manufacturing conglomerates, and infrastructure developers—without exposing their balance sheets to unacceptable volatility.

Capacity support is not just about writing larger individual risks. It also enables insurers to enter new lines of business, expand into previously underserved regions, and withstand the accumulation of claims from widespread events. In a developing market like Zimbabwe, where insurance penetration remains low but demand is growing, treaty reinsurance is a powerful enabler of market growth and financial inclusion.

## The Role of a Skilled Broker

Negotiating treaty reinsurance is a complex endeavour. It requires detailed actuarial analysis, a comprehensive understanding of local legal and regulatory frameworks, and established relationships with leading international and regional reinsurers. This is where a specialist reinsurance broker adds measurable value.

A skilled broker structures the treaty to maximise coverage while minimising cost, sources capacity from a diversified panel of reinsurers, and provides ongoing support through renewals, mid-term adjustments, and claims advocacy. Crucially, the broker bridges the gap between the local insurer and the global reinsurance market, ensuring that Zimbabwean risks are presented in a compelling and transparent manner.

For insurers seeking stability and sustainable growth, a well-designed treaty reinsurance programme—supported by an experienced broker—is not merely a financial tool. It is a strategic imperative.

Agriculture is the backbone of Zimbabwe's economy, employing a substantial portion of the population and contributing significantly to export earnings. Yet the sector remains acutely vulnerable to drought, cyclones, pests, and disease outbreaks. Agricultural insurance offers a pathway to resilience—and reinsurance makes that insurance possible.

## The Challenge of Agricultural Risk

Unlike property or motor insurance, where losses are largely independent, agricultural risks are highly correlated. A single drought can affect thousands of farmers simultaneously, generating claims that would overwhelm any primary insurer's capital base. This systemic nature of agricultural risk is the fundamental challenge that reinsurance is designed to address.

Reinsurers provide the capacity and stability needed for primary insurers to write agricultural portfolios with confidence. By ceding a substantial portion of their agricultural exposure to reinsurers, local insurers can offer meaningful coverage to farmers while protecting their solvency against catastrophic loss years.

## Reinsurance Structures for Agriculture

Agricultural reinsurance takes several forms, each suited to different types of exposure. Proportional treaties are common for crop insurance programmes, where the reinsurer shares a fixed percentage of premiums and losses. This structure provides consistent capacity and aligns the interests of the insurer and reinsurer over the long term.

For catastrophic drought or flood events, excess-of-loss treaties protect the insurer's aggregate loss experience above a defined attachment point. Weather-index products, which pay out automatically when rainfall or temperature deviates from specified parameters, are also gaining traction. These products benefit from parametric reinsurance structures that provide rapid claims settlement without the need for lengthy loss adjustment.

## Supporting Food Security and Rural Economies

The availability of agricultural insurance has profound implications beyond individual farm balance sheets. Insured farmers are more willing to invest in improved seeds, fertiliser, and irrigation equipment, knowing that their investment is protected against adverse weather. This increased investment drives higher productivity and contributes to national food security.

Reinsurance also enables insurers to expand into smallholder agriculture—a segment that has historically been underserved. With appropriate reinsurance support, insurers can develop microinsurance products tailored to small-scale farmers, with low premiums and simplified claims processes. This expansion supports rural livelihoods, reduces vulnerability to poverty, and strengthens the broader agricultural value chain.
